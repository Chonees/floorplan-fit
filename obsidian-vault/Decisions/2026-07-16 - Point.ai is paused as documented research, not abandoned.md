# Point.ai is paused as documented research, not abandoned

## Decision

Point.ai reached a boundary where further progress depended less on routine implementation and more on structured-CAD research.

The work crossed from ordinary product implementation into a research-heavy structured-CAD problem:

- training inputs and drawing styles produced domain-shift and consistency problems;
- improving mask metrics did not reliably improve visible or CAD quality;
- raster masks did not supply authoritative endpoints, shared junctions, closed loops, wall thickness, opening ownership, or editable CAD topology;
- a production-oriented continuation required separate wall/opening models, a typed wall graph, a global geometric/topological solver, an exact DXF renderer, CAD validation, representative benchmarks, and a larger proprietary gold-vector dataset.

A visually plausible output was not considered a sufficient demo. A credible demo needed to prove the complete editable-CAD contract, which materially increased the research time before trustworthy results.

## Product direction

The user confirmed that he performed the work individually and discussed this tradeoff with Carlos before continuing with FloorplanFit. Documentation must not attribute the work or decision to a team.

FloorplanFit reduced critical-path risk by starting from authoritative DXF geometry, persisting human-reviewed curation, and applying deterministic changes only within authorized zones.

Point.ai remains:

- documented;
- technically resumable;
- useful as the source of human-review, provenance, canonical-plan, site-fit, and auditable-mutation concepts;
- a candidate for future continuation when research time, benchmark data, and vector ground truth justify reopening the raster-to-CAD path.

No claim is made that a single contemporaneous pivot note exists; the technical causal account remains a synthesis of the documented failures, roadmap, data requirements, and the user's confirmed decision context.

## Editorial constraint

State the research boundary directly. Avoid defensive declarations such as "not a surrender" or claims about whether the developer had the ability to continue; the documented technical facts already establish that.
